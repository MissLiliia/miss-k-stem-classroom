// how we learn here

What Miss K's classroom looks like

Whether it's physics, computer science, or chemistry, my classroom runs on the same ideas: you learn science by doing science.

🔬

Hands-on, every week

Labs, builds, and experiments are the core of the course, not a treat. In my high school classes that meant 1–2 lab sessions every single week.

🧩

Projects & real cases

We learn through project-based and case-based learning — real problems that make you think, argue, and defend your reasoning with evidence.

🎮

Gamified learning

Points, quests, and friendly competition turn tough topics into challenges you actually want to beat. Engagement first, then mastery follows.

💻

Technology with purpose

Canvas, simulations, Python, and AI tools — used thoughtfully and ethically, so technology serves the learning instead of distracting from it.

🌱

Beyond the bell

I've founded and led student clubs — Garden Club, STEMinist Club, Scientific Research Club, Model UN — because curiosity shouldn't stop when class ends.

🤝

Families in the loop

Every Study Hub includes a family guide. Parents and guardians always know what we're learning, why, and how to help at home.

About Miss K

I'm a physics and computer science educator with two decades of teaching that started in Russia, grew in Maryland, USA, and now continues at BASIS International School Bangkok, where I teach Computer Science and Chemistry.

I hold an M.S. in Physics and Computer Science and an M.A. in Teaching, and I recently completed an Ed.S. in Instructional Technology (2025). I'm currently pursuing a PhD in Information Technology with a focus on Data Science — because I believe teachers should be learners first.

My philosophy is simple: the most important thing in my classroom is not my teaching — it's how my students are doing. I'm a student-centered teacher. Every lesson, project, and tool I use gets measured against one question: is this helping this student learn, grow, and feel capable? In Maryland, that focus showed in the numbers — my Honors Chemistry students' average final grades rose from 72% to 81% after I rebuilt the course around case-based learning, and Honors Physics enrollment tripled from one class period to three.

I also genuinely love to play with AI and new technology — experimenting, testing, and constantly modifying my teaching with any tool that makes learning better. For me, technology's real power is adaptivity: it lets me differentiate instruction so each student gets a path that fits how they learn, not a one-size-fits-all lesson.

Why I built this website

As a teacher — and as an instructional technology specialist — I kept seeing the same problem: information about a course lives in too many places. A syllabus here, a Canvas page there, a handout somewhere in a backpack. Students lose track, and families feel left out.

So I built one front door. Every one of my classes gets a complete Study Hub: what we're learning unit by unit, the key ideas in plain language, practice you can actually use, study strategies that work, and a guide written specifically for families.

This site is also my own learning in action. My graduate work is in instructional technology and data science, and I believe teachers should model what they teach — using technology deliberately, ethically, and in service of students. These pages are exactly that: technology built with purpose, for the people in my classroom.

If a student is curious at 9 p.m. on a Sunday, or a parent wants to know what "Unit 3" actually means — the answer should be one click away. Now it is.
